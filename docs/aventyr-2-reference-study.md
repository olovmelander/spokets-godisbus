# Äventyr 2 reference study: Billsta, Flärken and Olympia

Studied 4 November 2026 for two new Äventyr 2 worlds (a villa area by the river at Billsta in Själevad, and the
bathing place Flärkenbadet between Kubbe and Norrflärke) and for Olympia, the sports ground in Bredbyn. It
mirrors the [Bredbyn reference study](bredbyn-reference-study.md): sources, what each establishes, the visual
identity derived from them, and what the game should and should not claim.

**This study is text-led and no photograph was inspected.** This cloud session's network policy refused
`sv.wikipedia.org`, `commons.wikimedia.org`, `upload.wikimedia.org` and the municipal, tourism and map sites. Every
text source below was therefore read through search-engine extracts of the page, not by opening the page itself.
No image was downloaded, so there are no byte counts or SHA-256 hashes. The candidate photographs listed under
[Image candidates](#image-candidates-not-inspected) are records of what exists, for a later session or Olov's
computer to retrieve and look at. Nothing here comes from a dated 2026 site visit.

Each section separates **verified** statements (stated by the cited source) from **inference** (my reading,
general knowledge, or design suggestion). Distances are straight-line values computed from public locality
reference points (Wikipedia, Wikidata, GeoNames and a bathing-site listing), rounded to the kilometre. They are
not road or river distances.

Reference photographs, if retrieved later, stay outside the repository and the game packs (see
[CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). Study shapes and colours; make original geometry and materials.

### What Billsta is (verified, and the limits)

- Billsta is a small tätort (about 360 people) in Själevad parish, on **Billstasundet**, where Moälven leaves
  Happstafjärden and enters Själevadsfjärden. Billaberget rises beside the road there. Road 348 passes through.
- Själevad's church sits at the other, downstream end of Själevadsfjärden, at Själevadssundet, where two bridges
  (one of them the E4's) cross; the church village is about **5 km south-east** of Billsta. Örnsköldsvik's
  centre is about 11 km east-south-east.
- The farm names Billsta, Överbilla and Främmerbilla, and a 1712 record of the farm "Billsta 5" in a genealogy
  source ([billberg.info](http://www.billberg.info/page-153.html)), show an old farming village.

**Not found:** any source describing Billsta's houses: their era, whether they are detached villas, terraced
houses or flats, a playground, a sledging hill, a jetty or a bathing place. Billsta has no bathing place in the
municipal lists found; Käringbadet is the bathing place in Själevad.

### Inference

- An old riverside farm village with later detached houses is the likely mix, judging by the farm names, the
  population and the road. Typical houses in Örnsköldsvik's villa areas, generally, are one- or one-and-a-half
  storey timber houses from the 1950s to 1970s with painted panel walls, pitched roofs, woodsheds and big lawns.
  This is general regional knowledge, **not** a description of Billsta.
- The water at Billsta is a narrow, sheltered strait between two reed-lined lakes, not open sea. Both lakes are
  "fjärdar" of the river, not of the Baltic.
- On a high point the church is likely to be visible down the lake: the panorama *Moälven 12* (below) is
  described as showing Veckefjärden, the church and Happstafjärden in one view. Whether it can be seen from
  Billsta itself is unverified.

### Visual identity for a Billsta world

- **The narrow and the reeds.** A strait where the river's current is visible between two calm, wide lakes;
  reed beds along both shores (verified: reed-rich, nutrient-rich lake).
- **Billaberget behind.** A steep south-facing cliff with ledges, a big boulder field at its foot, and hazel
  bushes among the boulders (verified). It gives the world its skyline and a vertical climb.
- **The white octagon across the water.** The church's eight white walls, a lantern storey and a small spired
  turret, on a height at the far end of the lake (verified form). It is the far landmark, as Anundsjö's tower is in
  Byn.
- **A villa garden by the shore** (inference, composite, never a real house): lawn sloping to reeds, a woodshed
  with stacked firewood, a rowing boat turned over on trestles for the winter, a small jetty, garden furniture
  stacked under a tarp, apple and rowan trees, a road with mailboxes, a bus shelter.
- **October palette:** dark water, straw-coloured reeds, birch and aspen mostly bare, the last yellow leaves on
  the ground, rowan berries, dark spruce on the hill, white church walls catching lightning.

### What Flärkenbadet is (verified)

- The lake is called **Flärken** (no source uses "Flärkesjön"). The bathing place, **Flärkenbadet**, is in
  Norrflärke, run by Kubbe-Norrflärke IF, the two villages' shared club.
- The canoe trail on Norra Anundsjöån passes it. It follows that Flärken is a widening of **Norra Anundsjöån**,
  the river that goes on south to Bredbyn. An old place-name text calls a Flarken on the river a shallow lake.
- It has: a bay; jetties; a diving tower; changing rooms; dry toilets; barbecue places; a rest area and picnic
  places; a volleyball court; a playground; parking about 1 km off road 348.
- Kubbe lies just north-west of it and Norrflärke runs south of it along the river, in a farmed valley; the river
  is what separates the two villages. The badplats lies about **6 km north** of Bredbyn.

**Not found:** sand or grass at the shore, the diving tower's height, a raft (*badflotte*), swimming buoys, a sauna,
the forest type around the lake, or any openly licensed photograph.

### Inference

- "Sjösatta igen" each spring means the jetties and tower are put back in the water every season. They are
  probably floating sections, taken out or pulled ashore in autumn. **In late October they may lie stacked on
  the shore.** Olov can confirm.
- A family bath that has volleyball, a playground and a "badvik" very likely has a sandy or grassy shore. That is
  unverified.
- The forest around is probably the region's usual mix of spruce, pine and birch, with farmland and hay barns on
  the valley floor (consistent with the newsletter's "odlingslandskap"). Unverified for this lake.
- Beaver signs (gnawed stems, a lodge) fit the canoe trail's description, but none is recorded at the bath.

### Visual identity for a Flärken world

- **A sheltered bay on a river lake**, wooded hills around and the dark valley behind.
- **The diving tower** as the vertical landmark, with **jetties** (in the water, or stacked ashore in October).
- **A small wooden building or two** (changing rooms, a dry toilet), picnic tables, a ring of barbecue stones,
  a volleyball net, a small playground.
- **Club volunteer feel:** practical, hand-kept, wooden. No signs, crests or lettering.

### What Olympia is (verified)

A combined sports ground in Bredbyn: Anundsjö IF's football ground with an **artificial-turf main pitch**, a
**B pitch** and a **7-a-side pitch**, and a **ski stadium** with start and finish, **floodlit trails** and a

**Not found:** the year it was built or why it is called Olympia; whether the main pitch has floodlights, a stand,

### Inference

- A small Norrland ground of this kind usually has a low covered stand or terraced bank, a wooden or steel
  kiosk/clubhouse, team benches with clear roofs, a chain-link fence and ball-stop nets behind the goals, and
  floodlight masts. All of this is generic until a photograph confirms it.
- "Hockey" at the club suggests a winter rink (frozen or flooded) nearby; in late October there would be none.
- Nothing in October: no snow, so the ski stadium shows only its start/finish area, trail entrances and lights.

### Ten football puzzles and set pieces for a 15 cm boy on a flooded pitch at night

Scale (standard measurements, general knowledge; 1 EL ≈ 15 cm): a size-5 ball is about 22 cm across (≈1.5 EL);
a full goal is 7.32 × 2.44 m (≈49 × 16 EL); a corner flag is at least 1.5 m (≈10 EL); artificial grass blades
are 4–6 cm (≈0.3–0.4 EL), knee-high to him; goal-net mesh is typically 10–12 cm (≈0.7 EL), a ladder for him.

1. **Bollen flyter** (push puzzle): roll a stray ball down the slope by the benches into the flooded goalmouth,
   where it floats and becomes the stepping stone over a gap, the bark-raft rule with a ball ("Bollar flyter, precis
   som Klonk!").
2. **Run the ball** (ride): balanced on top of a floating ball, Elof walks to keep it rolling across a big puddle;
   stop walking and it stops.
3. **The goal net** (climb): the net is a soft ladder up to the crossbar; rain makes it sag and bounce like a
   hammock (bouncer), and the crossbar is a 16 EL high walkway with a view of the whole pitch.
4. **The corner flag in the storm** (gust and moving platform): the flag snaps back and forth in the gusts; time
   a jump onto the cloth as it swings towards the fence.
5. **Training cones** (stairs and shelter): a toppled stack of cones makes a slide; upright cones are shelters from
   gusts across the open pitch, like the flower pots in Stormgården.
6. **The benches** (drips and shelter): water drips from the edge of the clear bench roofs (read their shadows);
   under them is a dry path.
7. **The ball-stop net and fence** (climb): the high net behind the goal is a long climb with a lightning view of
   the village; the chain-link's diamonds are handholds.
8. **The family kick** (call spot): the world's family helper is called and gently kicks the ball, and Elof
   rides it across the pitch, like Bertil's boot boat; the ball stops in the far goal: a goal in the storm, with a
   soft cheer sound.
9. **The biathlon targets** (puzzle, shown as targets only): five black discs. Elof flicks pine cones at them;
   each one turns white with a *klonk*, and all five lower the next platform. Only target plates and the mat;
   nothing else from the range is shown.
10. **The dark floodlights** (lightning set piece): the masts are dark because the power is out; each flash shows the
    whole flooded pitch and the white centre circle for a moment, which is how Elof sees where the trail goes next.

## 4. Geography and the route

### Verified chain of water

From Flärken the water runs **south down Norra Anundsjöån** through Norrflärke and Nysjön (a dam) to **Bredbyn**,
where it meets Södra Anundsjöån just above **Anundsjösjön** ([Bredbyn study](bredbyn-reference-study.md)). Below
the lake the river is called **Moälven**. It falls steeply past Anundsjö power station and Mellansel, calms below
Gottne, runs past Moliden and Gala, and widens into **Happstafjärden**. Through the narrow **Billstasundet at
Billsta** it enters **Själevadsfjärden**, leaves through Själevadssundet by **Själevad's church**, crosses
Veckefjärden and meets the sea at Domsjö.

So **Flärken, Bredbyn and Billsta lie on one water route**, and road 348 links all three over land.

| From → to | Straight-line distance | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bredbyn → Flärkenbadet | ≈ 6 km | north |
| Bredbyn → Kubbe | ≈ 8 km | north-north-west |
| Flärkenbadet → Billsta | ≈ 30 km | south-east |
| Bredbyn → Billsta | ≈ 25 km | south-east |
| Billsta → Själevad church village | ≈ 5 km | south-east, down the lake |
| Billsta → Örnsköldsvik centre | ≈ 11 km | east-south-east |

### The route a child would understand (inference)

The storm wind blows the figure **up the valley** to the bathing place at Flärken. There it falls into the water,
and **the river carries it home past Bredbyn and down the big river to the sea**: through the narrow strait at
Billsta, where the white church stands at the end of the lake. Wood floats, which is already the Sjön world's
rule.

The weather also supports it: an autumn storm's wind can blow from the south ahead of a front and turn north-west
behind it, so "vinden vände" sends the figure north first and then south-east with the river. This is general
weather knowledge and is not needed for the story.

The real river passes a power-station dam and a steep reach below Anundsjön. Show the journey as a map card or a
thought picture (a blue line with the three places), not as a literal ride through a dam.

## Translation into the game

### Billsta world: a villa area by the river at night in the storm

1. **The strait's current** (ride): a birch leaf or bark piece carried through Billstasundet, with reed stems to
   grab at the edges.
2. **Reed beds** as climbable stems and swaying platforms (the existing reed and tussock kit).
3. **The overturned rowing boat on trestles**: climb the hull like a roof; the tarp over it flaps (bouncer).
4. **The woodshed and its stack of firewood**: logs as steps, the gaps as shelter, a cut stump as a plinth.
5. **Stacked garden furniture under a tarp**: a climbing tower, the tarp's flapping corner a moving platform.
6. **The mailbox row by the road**: lids that flap open in gusts as moving ledges (no names on them).
7. **The bus shelter** on the empty road: the dry place in a gust run, with rain sheeting off its roof (drips).
8. **The small jetty** at the shore, waves washing over in gusts; shelter behind its posts (as Sjön).
9. **Billaberget's boulder field and cliff ledges**: the big vertical climb of the world, with hazel bushes among
   the boulders and **hazelnuts** on the ground in October (Klonk could crack one: *klonk*).
10. **An otter** (the river's Natura 2000 animal) as the world's kind helper: it nudges Elof's bark across the
    current, as the owl flies him in Stormskogen.
11. **The white octagonal church** at the far end of the lake, lit by lightning: the distant goal, the place the
    old trägubbe is heading for.
12. **A lit window** in a garden by the water: the warm goal of the world. This is a composite house, never a real
    one.
13. **Apples and rowan berries** shaken down by the wind (drips), apples rolling on the slope to the shore.
14. **The garden hose reel or a water butt** (a turn spot), reusing Stormgården's tap puzzle in a new form.

### Flärken world: the bathing place at night in the storm

1. **The diving tower**: ladder climb to the top platform, the world's lookout; each lightning flash shows the
   river's outlet and the figure drifting towards it.
2. **Jetty sections stacked ashore** for the winter (if Olov confirms): a giant staircase of planks; in the water,
   floating sections bob (movers with a cycle).
3. **The changing-room door banging in the wind**: a moving platform and a timing gate; coat hooks inside as ledges.
4. **The dry toilet**: a small wooden hut, the door's cut-out window as the way in (a gentle joke, no more).
5. **The volleyball net**: a bouncy wall to climb or a trampoline when it sags with rain; the sand court is a beach
   of big grains.
6. **The playground** (verified): swings swinging in the gusts as moving platforms, a slide as a ride, a sandbox.
7. **Picnic tables**: tops as high platforms, the space beneath as shelter.
8. **The barbecue ring**: a ring of stones and a grill grate over it, a bridge of bars (as Byn's grate).
9. **The life-ring on its post** (inference, a common fitting): unhook it and it floats out as a raft.
10. **Canoes on a rack or upturned on the grass** (canoe trail): a shelter, then one launched down the river is the
    way to the next world.
11. **A beaver's gnawed stems and lodge** on the shore: a climbable mound, the beaver asleep inside.
12. **The village footbridge** (30 m, built by the villagers): seen across the river in a flash, swaying in the
    storm; a set piece if the route needs a crossing.
13. **The river's outlet**: the current at the lake's end, where the figure is carried away: the reason to follow.
14. **Hay barns** in the fields behind, dark shapes in lightning; a gap in a barn's boarding is a shelter.

### Olympia (if it becomes a set piece in Byn i stormen)

Use the ten football ideas above. Keep the ground generic: plain-coloured boards, a plain clock or scoreboard
without lettering, plain team benches. Its identity is the **combination**: a football pitch beside a ski
stadium's start area, trail lights going into the forest, and a 1950s feel.

## What the game should and should not claim

**Should:**
- Use Billstasundet, Billaberget's cliff and boulders, reeds, and the white octagonal church across the lake as the
  Billsta world's identity.
- Use the diving tower, jetties, changing huts, volleyball court, playground and barbecue places as Flärkenbadet's.
- Tie the worlds together by the water: Flärken's river flows past Bredbyn to Billsta.
- Use the club's everyday kit at Olympia (artificial turf, goals, nets, cones, benches, trail lights) with no marks.

**Should not:**
- Model, place or describe any real house in Billsta, or imply which house is the grandparents'. The garden is a
  composite.
- Name Billsta, Flärken or Olympia in player text before Olov decides. Äventyr 2's canon names only "byn" and
  "sjön", and the grandparents are not on the consent list.

*Settled later the same day:* Olov asked for Billsta, Flärken and Olympia by name and sent a picture of mormor and
morfar's house. The game names the three places; its house is a loose interpretation in plain shapes of that
picture, never placed, numbered or addressed, and the picture is not committed. The consent question stays open
for Olov ("Frågor till Olov" in `HANDOVER.md`).
- Show club crests, sponsor boards, business signs, the PostNord stamp or any lettering.
- Refer to the real September 2025 flood at Kubbe, in which cellars flooded and a bridge was threatened. The game's
  storm is friendly, and the flood was real for the villagers.
- Claim survey accuracy, house eras, the diving tower's height, a sandy beach or Olympia's stands and lights. None
  of these was verified.
- Show the river journey as a ride through a dam or power station.

building, a session with access to `sv.wikipedia.org`, `commons.wikimedia.org` and `upload.wikimedia.org` (Olov's
computer, or a cloud environment with those hosts allowed) should:

  file's byte count and SHA-256 in a manifest like [bredbyn-reference-sources.json](bredbyn-reference-sources.json),
  and keep the originals outside git;
- find a recent openly licensed view of Flärkenbadet and of Olympia, or ask Olov for his own photographs (which
  stay in `photos/`).
