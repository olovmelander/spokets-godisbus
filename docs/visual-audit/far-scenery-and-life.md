# Visual audit: the far scenery, and the life in it

> One of seven audits made on 5 October 2026: see [the overview](../visual-audit.md). The auditor looked at
> 20 to 30 pictures of the running game at 1180×820 and 844×390, on High and on Low, with stand-in figures.
> Names in brackets such as `b03` or `p17` are the auditor's working pictures, which are not kept. File names
> are as they were that morning: `src/render/dressing.ts` has since become `src/render/dressing/`, a module
> for each place. Line numbers are of that day.

## The first second

A stranger sees soft, well-coloured depth behind the path: green-gold columns in the forest, dark spruces against cream mist in the bog, violet ridges under a peach sky on the mountain. That softness, the palette of each place and the parallax are good and must be kept. What lets it down most is that the far world is a stage set with nobody in it: nothing moves but clouds at 2 to 7 pixels a second, and every shape is one of six stock stamps (a sine hill, a triangle spruce, a lollipop pine, a blob, a column, a band) that could stand anywhere on earth. A second look finds three things the art bible promises that are not in the picture at all (the valley's lit windows, the valley under the flight, the far village's walls), and two bad joins: the bog's water ends in a hard corner against the mist, and the summit looks up at mountains, not out over them.

## The five to do first

**1. Free the draw calls (row 2).**
- Build `src/render/quads.ts`: a batch of quads in one `BufferGeometry` with position, uv and RGBA colour, static or dynamic. Then, in this order: `foreground()`'s tufts into one static batch and its shrubs into another (one atlas each, quads sorted by z once); the forest's shafts into one dynamic batch (each shaft's breathing goes into its vertices' alpha); the bog's sheets into one; the windows' bars and panes at home.
- Done when the pictures at granskog 96, garden 108 and myren 83 are the same to the eye as `granskog-096-phone.png`, `garden-108-phone.png` and `myren-083-phone.png`, and the printed draw calls on a phone are about 86, 73 and 75 where they are 100, 84 and 82.
- What could go wrong: a batch is never culled, so a chapter's 125 quads are always sent (250 triangles: nothing). Soft cards blend, so the order inside a batch matters: sort far to near. The shrubs take the scene's fog and the tufts do not: keep them two materials. On Low, check that the material grade still reaches them.

**2. Put back what is hidden (rows 4, 5 and 8).**
- The far village: `renderOrder −3` and 2 EL up. The valley's windows: out of `fell()`, and into the dusk's life once item 3 exists (until then, repaint them on the z −64 card at rows 160 to 168). The bog's water: run it the chapter's length behind z −6 and fade it out by z −44.
- Done when byn 8 shows yellow, white and red walls with white gable boards under the roofs, as in the lower half of `byn-008-order-pair.png`; when norrsken 18 and 30 show a cluster of warm lights just over the horizon at both sizes; when myren 83 has no line and no corner where the water meets the mist.
- What could go wrong: the far village's walls then stand in front of the hills at full colour, so check its painted haze (0.25 to 0.62) is enough. The nearest slope's spruces can cover the lights: move the cluster along the picture until both places show it. The water change touches the caustics' limits (`causticWorld.z > -46.0`).

**3. The life batch, its first cast, then the moose (row 1; the last section).**
- In this order: the plan (pure) and its tests; the batch; the cast drawn by the atlas builder (cranes over the bog, smoke over the far village, a shooting star and the valley's lights in the finale, geese); the stages in the chapters; then `art/blender/life.py`, the moose's cells, and the moose in the bog.
- Done when, standing at the bog's stage, a moose 27 px at the shoulder on a phone (55 on a tablet) walks out from behind a spruce of the nearest card, its legs lost in the mist, stops, lifts its head, and walks on; when it is plainly beyond the spruces and paler than them; when the draw calls rise by one while it is there and by none after.
- What could go wrong: a child runs toward it or waits for it to do something: see "How a child is told". The script budget is 450 KB with 391 used: the system must stay under 6 KB gzipped, so no `SkinnedMesh` and no `AnimationMixer`. The atlas must be installed as `boot/candy` is, so that the two warm-up frames follow.

**4. The summit (rows 3, 10 and 15).**
- Thin ridges, the stronger `sink`, `outlook` for the finale, the painted slope and valley under the crests, and the hour that moves.
- Done when berget 108 shows sky over the top 40% of the picture, six or more thin bands of ridge within 10% of the height over the horizon, each paler, and spruce tops just over the granite's edge; when the flight at x 36 shows the slope and a lake below, not a flat colour; when norrsken 2 and berget 149 show the same horizon.
- What could go wrong: thin far ridges lose the big violet shapes that make berget's picture now. Keep one long back with a knob taller than the rest (`tall` 20) as the picture's anchor, and judge it on the phone first.

**5. The trees and the open mire (rows 6, 7 and 12).**
- The four stamps, then the bog's two nearest cards rebuilt with them, then the ridge with a spruce skyline, then the same stamps in the garden's edge and the far village.
- Done when myren 4 and 83 show the mountain and the far shore through an open middle, slim spires in one group, small crooked pines, and three silver snags that can be found on a phone; and when no two spruces in a picture have the same outline.
- What could go wrong: thin spruces and small pines carry less dark against the mist, and that contrast is the bog's picture. Keep the group of three big spruces, and keep one blur step.

## Keep

- **The layer machine:** `picture()` with its halve-and-double blur, `around()` for joining, haze mixed into the colours, the clear top row, `hold` and `sink`, and the five tests in `tests/unit/backdrop.test.ts`. It costs no download and a fixed four or five draw calls.
- **Code-drawn far pictures.** Do not replace them with plates rendered in Blender (§2.8 asks for it). A far card is magnified 4.5 to 6.9 times on a tablet and blurred on purpose: a rendered plate would buy little there, cost 25 to 50 KB each (my estimate), and bring a joining problem. Spend Blender on the moose and the mid-ground.
- **The softness.** Far things out of focus is the macro idea itself. Keep at least one blur step on every far card, and make the life as soft.
- **The forest's four depths:** columns, boughs, young spruces and spots of light. Add to them (rows 1 and 20); change nothing.
- **The bog's colours:** cream mist #f6ecd4 behind dark spruce green, the mist band at each card's foot, the blue-violet mountain. Rows 6 and 7 change shapes, not colours.
- **The mountain's and the dusk's colour scripts,** the stars as round points at any picture shape, and one nightfall shared by sky, cards and haze.
- **Nothing to read on a far house:** no window, door, number or sign. Row 11 keeps that.
- **The morning windows at home.**

Not in the cast: **swallows.** As far as I know they have left Ångermanland by mid-September, and the game is in late September and October. Thrushes, geese and the magpie take their place over the lawn. Cranes are in season.

**A bolder moose, for Olov to say yes or no to.** The forest's far cards are truly near: 5 to 12 m. A moose passing there at its true size would be 14 EL at the shoulder, 40% of the picture's height: four legs like moving trunks between the trunks, the body up in the boughs, pale in the mist. It is the game's own idea (a tiny boy in a big world) and could be its most remembered picture. It could also frighten. Build the far small one first.

### How often, and when not

Three kinds, so that it stays a gift:
- **Always:** small, slow and pale: smoke, the valley's windows, midges. Under 2% of the picture.
- **Now and then:** a flock, a V, a hare. One at a time, at least 25 s after the last, each on stage 8 to 40 s. Two or three in a chapter.
- **Once:** the moose, the capercaillie, the reindeer, the shooting star. One in a chapter.

A happening belongs to a **stage:** a stretch a chapter names (`life: [{ kind, from, to }]`), where the far view is open and the trail is calm. A good stage begins just after a big candy, where a child stops anyway, with no hook, ledge, hidden candy, soft tussock or family member within 8 EL. It starts when the camera enters the stage and he is on his feet; never in a chapter's first 8 s, on a ride, in a bubble, on a hose, during a family picture, while the bog's mist is up or a gust blows. If it cannot start, it waits for his next visit. Candidates, for the chapters' owners to check: cranes after myren's checkpoint at 34, the moose after 96.5; the hare after granskog's at 27, the moose after 150; the reindeer after berget's at 108.

### How a child is told it is scenery

- **It is made of the far card's stuff.** One colour mixed with the place's haze, as soft as the card, no outline, no eye-shine, no sparkle. Sparkle and red, pink and gold are the candy's and the hook's. A test holds the tints to that.
- **Something always passes in front of it:** the nearest spruces or trunks, and the mist over its legs. What is behind a tree is beyond the tree.
- **It answers nothing.** No button, no call and no jump changes what it does. The camera never turns or zooms to it. No bubble, no hint, no album page, no counter.
- **It goes the other way.** It walks against his direction or across, never ahead along the trail as a guide would, and never stops over a gap, a hook or a secret.
- **The things to use look different:** the ladybird, the jay and the crane stand on the play plane, sharp, in colour, with the game's diamond over them.
- **It leaves by itself.** Nothing is lost by walking on, and nothing more comes from waiting.
- **Its sound is far:** one quiet, wordless call (a crane, a drumming, a croak), under the candy's sounds.
- **If it is still chased** when Olov tests: let the ghost look at it for a second and then go on. That is how a parent says "look", and then "come".

### The system

- **Data** (`src/content/life.ts`, no three, no DOM): each kind's cells, frames a second, size in EL, speed (always its stride divided by its cycle, so that no hoof slides), tint; each place's slots (`z`, `hold`, how much haze, how much of its height the mist takes) and its always-there members.
- **The plan** (`src/render/life-plan.ts`, pure): from the place, the chapter's stages, the camera's x, the clock, whether he is busy and whether "calm" is set, it returns the quads to draw: where, which cell, which colour and alpha. It keeps which once-kinds this visit has shown. The clock is the view's, so a pause stops everything.
- **The batch** (`src/render/life.ts`): one `Mesh`, 32 quads, `MeshBasicMaterial({ map, vertexColors: true, transparent: true, depthWrite: false, fog: false })`, `renderOrder −3` like the far cards, at its slot's `z`, so it sorts between two cards by depth. A quad stands at `cameraX + (p − cameraX × hold)`, at the eye line less the slot's `sink`. `visible` is false while it has no quads: no draw call. `dress()` builds it; `Dressing.update()` gets one more argument, `quiet`.
- **One draw call** for all of a place's life. The forest has two slots (behind and in front of its far trunks), never both on stage, so still one at a time. Garden, byn and norrsken have theirs always on (smoke, windows): they are light places.
- **The atlas:** one picture of 1024×512 for the whole cast, carried as the texture of `art/baked/boot/life.glb`, so `npm run assets` makes it KTX2 with no new step. It is installed as `boot/candy` is, and the two warm-up frames follow. Until it arrives the batch has a clear 1×1 map and nothing is on stage.
- **How the haze takes it:** three things. Its tint is mixed with the place's haze by its slot (bog 0.25, forest 0.5, ridge 0.4). A walker is two quads: the upper 65% whole, the lower 35% fading to 0.2 at the hooves, so the legs go into the mist and no hoof has to meet any ground. And the card in front is drawn after it: the bog's nearest card lays its own mist band (0.5 at its thickest, from 2.3 EL over the eye line to 3.3 under) across the moose, which leaves it a mid-grey shape on cream.
- **Tiers:** the same on Low, Mid and High: at most 32 unlit quads. With "calm" or reduced motion: the always-there members and the slow walkers only; no burst, no streak.
- **A switch for pictures and tests:** `&life=moose` puts that happening on stage at once.

**The moose: the sprite sheet.** It is the one animal Olov named, so it will be looked at, and on a tablet it is 55 to 110 px tall, where a cut-out shows its hinges. Its whole character is in things a cut-out cannot give: the shoulder hump rolling, long legs crossing each other, the heavy head turning to look. It costs one small picture and no code. Birds, smoke, lights and the streak are dots and chevrons: draw those.

**How it is built.** `art/blender/life.py`, from numbers like `candy.py` and `jay.py`: nothing bought, downloaded or generated by an AI, photographs only looked at. The animal is a skeleton of about 50 points joined by edges, with a Skin modifier and two subdivisions: no armature and no weights. For each frame a gait function moves the points and the skin follows. The camera looks from the side without perspective, the film is clear, the hide is one dark colour, and one warm sun from above and a little behind puts light on the back and antlers.

**How its legs move.** A slow walk in which each side's hind hoof lands just before its fore hoof: the phases are hind left 0, fore left 0.2, hind right 0.5, fore right 0.7. A hoof stands for 62% of the cycle and travels back under the body at walking speed; for the other 38% it swings forward and lifts a quarter of the leg's length, high, as a moose does in a bog. The middle joint is found from the top joint and the hoof by two bones of fixed length: the fore leg's bends forward, the hind leg's back. The body rises and falls a little twice a cycle, the head nods a little after it, the bell swings. Twelve frames at ten a second: a cycle of 1.2 s and a stride of 1.35 EL at the bog's size, so 1.1 EL/s. Because the far legs hide behind the near ones, frames 7 to 12 repeat 1 to 6 unless the far pair is rendered paler: do that, it gives depth for free.

**The probe.** `probe/moose_probe.py`, run as `blender -b --factory-startup --python`, outside the repository: 12 frames in 1.7 s on the processor, the lowest covered row the same in every frame to one pixel. Its moose reads as a moose (hump, deep chest, long legs, heavy nose, bell, palms) and is still a sketch: the skin creases at the shoulder, the legs are thin, it has no standing or turning frames.

**How it is tested without a browser.**
- `tests/unit/life.test.ts`, on the pure plan: every slot lies between two of `farLayers(place)` in depth, and holds between theirs or the same as the card it stands on; over ten simulated minutes at three walking speeds there are never more than 32 quads, never two happenings at once, never less than 25 s between them, and none starts while he is busy; each walker's speed times its cycle is its stride to 1%; anything that must be recognised is at least 12 px tall at 844×390, by the camera's own formula; no tint is redder than the rule allows; "calm" leaves only the slow ones.
- `tests/unit/life-sheet.test.ts`, on `art/baked/boot/life.json`, which the generator writes beside the picture: every kind in the cast has its cells; in a walk the lowest covered row is the same in every frame to one pixel; the last frame is no further from the first than neighbours are from each other; a cell is 15 to 45% covered and nothing touches its edge; the hooves' travel while they stand matches the stride in the data.
- A chapter test: every stage is calm ground by the rule above.
- The robot is untouched: life is not in the simulation, and that proves it cannot change play.
- The browser smoke test, with `&life=moose`: one draw call more, no shader program more.
