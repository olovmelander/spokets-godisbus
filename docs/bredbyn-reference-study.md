# Bredbyn reference study for Byn

Studied 10 October 2026. Olov asked for Byn to be recognisable as Bredbyn, especially
Anundsjö kyrka and Köpmangatan. Eight actual photographs were downloaded and visually
inspected. The newest inspected street photograph is from **2021**, not 2026; the
church photographs are from **2012**. This is a reference-led interpretation, not
a claim to have surveyed the present village.

The [source manifest](bredbyn-reference-sources.json) records each original URL,
photographer, date, licence, byte count and SHA-256. Session copies are outside git
in `/workspace/scratch/bredbyn-references/`. Reference photographs must remain
outside the public repository and game packs, as required by [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).
Study the architecture and landscape; make original geometry and materials. Do not
use photographs as textures, painted plates or embedded documentation images.

## Architectural cues to preserve

**Church and tower form a pair of different silhouettes.** Keep the church as a
long white-plastered volume with a very steep brown shingle roof. Tall, narrow,
round-headed windows have pale mullions. The small projecting gabled porch breaks
the long wall. The ridge carries small ornaments, including a slender central
spindle. These features are visible across the four church photographs.

The free-standing tower is dark timber. Its broad lower body meets a flared
shingle roof; above that is a taller panelled belfry with curved decorative braces.
Another rounded roof supports the square clock lantern. A swollen shingled crown
then tapers into a fine weather vane. The dark clock face has a pale rim and hands.
This layered outline should read even when the model is small on a phone.

[Svenska kyrkan's parish description](https://www.svenskakyrkan.se/ornskoldsvikssodra/anundsjo-skorped/anundsjo-kyrka)
identifies the church as medieval and dates the separate, 20-metre bell tower to
1759. Its page was updated in May 2026; that confirms the building identity and
history, not a fresh photographic survey. Simplified in-game proportions may be
adjusted for legibility, while retaining the separated white nave and dark tower.

**Köpmangatan gets its identity from variety and street scale.** The 2021 view shows
warm yellow horizontal boarding, a sage-green vertically boarded gable, red timber,
white trims, contrasting roof colours, window hoods and occasional small awnings.
Buildings sit close to narrow raised pavements. The street falls towards a wooded
horizon. Use these colour families, varied rooflines and rhythms in original
facades; omit the photographed business identities.

The older 2012 view adds pale blue and cream facades, brick among the timber and
plaster, shop glazing beneath smaller upper windows, striped awnings and slender
streetlights with curved arms. It supports a varied small-town shopping street,
not a uniform row of red cottages. Shop occupancy, signs and paint can have changed
since the photograph; they are not a current business inventory.

## Landscape and atmosphere

The 2020 Storklocken photograph shows broad rounded forested hills behind a low
valley. The church and tower appear above the trees as recognisable markers. Use
layered, subdued ridges and openings between trees so the settlement feels nestled
in this terrain. The spring picture is evidence for shape and relationship, not
for Byn's October foliage or morning colour.

[Länsstyrelsen's river project description](https://www.lansstyrelsen.se/download/18.57b7270718c8acb32b774820/1705072908112/Projektbeskrivning%20Norra%20Anundsj%C3%B6%C3%A5n%20avrinningsomr%C3%A5de.pdf)
places the meeting of Norra and Södra Anundsjöån at Bredbyn, just upstream of
Anundsjösjön, and describes a steep final reach. The regional river photograph
supports dark reflective water broken by small pale foam bands, rounded rocks and
birch/conifer banks. It does **not** establish the exact appearance or location of
the game's crossing. Preserve the existing playable crossing and treat its scenery
as an interpretation until a confirmed village-river reference is available.

## Translation into the game

- Reveal the distinct church-and-tower pair across an open churchyard; keep both
  silhouettes clear of foreground trees and the game's controls.
- Let the shopping street read through varied facade heights, gables, pale trims,
  awnings, broad shop windows, pavement edges and planted gaps.
- Use an original church shingle pattern and painted timber colours; avoid a
  photographic surface pasted onto geometry.
- Keep the familiar chapter title **Byn**, existing route and interaction positions.
  Architecture belongs behind the readable candy trail and prompts.
- Retain Byn's cool October-morning sky, warm low sunlight and warm shop windows.
  Photograph lighting is reference context, not a requirement to change story time.

Review landscape and portrait views at gameplay scale. The tower's outline, the
white nave and the differing street facades should be recognisable without reading
a label. Check that the decorative street and churchyard never resemble usable
paths through a collision barrier or hide Elof, candy or the ghost.

## Authored assets and rebuilding

The cloud implementation uses original geometry and vertex colours in
[`bredbyn-landmarks.ts`](../art/procedural/bredbyn-landmarks.ts) and
[`bredbyn-street.ts`](../art/procedural/bredbyn-street.ts). The church and separate
bell tower are two meshes; six varied street facades form two more. The low yard
boundary, warm yellow timber front, pale plaster bakery and rounded wooded valley
frame these distant buildings. Proportions and placement serve the existing game
route and phone view; they are not survey measurements.

From the repository root, with dependencies installed and Node 22.12 or newer:

```sh
node --experimental-strip-types scripts/bake-bredbyn.mjs
npm run assets
```

The first command writes `art/baked/boot/bredbyn.glb` using three's GLTFExporter.
The second runs the normal asset packing pipeline, including meshopt compression
and manifest/GPU estimates, and requires the project's KTX-Software installation.
Do not regenerate packs while a browser review is using them.

This is a procedural cloud asset pass. The existing near-street kit remains the
Blender-authored `art/baked/boot/village.glb`; the new Bredbyn bank does not claim
Blender modelling or baked lighting. Further hand-modelled detail and material
refinement can use the established local Blender workflow on Olov's computer,
with these source records and the ignored photograph collection. The reference
photographs themselves are never build inputs or shipped textures.

## Limits and next reference needs

This study does not have a dated 2026 streetscape survey, measurements, a verified
village-rapids viewpoint or an exact map reconstruction. The 2012 and 2021 street
views are evidence from their stated years. Historical postcards were not used as
evidence of current appearance. A 2022 Flickr church album was located, but its
photographs could not be retrieved and were not used for visual conclusions.

Olov's existing ignored `photos/landscape/` collection remains useful for a later
Blender refinement. For further realism, compare the game with a recent public
view of the church/tower together and both directions along Köpmangatan. Keep
reference originals private to the working copy and preserve their source records.

To retrieve an original again, open its source-page link and choose **Original
file**, or use `original_url` from the manifest. Save it outside the repository,
then compare its SHA-256 with the recorded value before using it for review.
